1. What SpaceWise is

SpaceWise looks at a drive or a folder and tells you what is using the space, then helps you get some of it back. It works entirely on your own computer.

It scans a folder, draws what is inside as a map of blocks sized by how much space each thing takes, and lets you look closer. When there is something worth clearing, it builds a plan, shows you exactly what it would do, and moves what you approve to the Recycle Bin — where you can still get it back.

What it deliberately does not do

It does not touch the registry. It does not disable startup programs, stop services or "optimise" your PC. It does not clean anything on a schedule or in the background. It does not claim to make your computer faster.

Nothing is deleted without you approving that specific item, and there is no "clean everything" button. That button is the reason tools like this lose people's work.

It works with no internet connection. Scanning, analysing and cleaning up never need one.

2. Installing it

The installer is SpaceWise-Setup-0.1.0-x64.exe (about 107 MB).

  1. Double-click the installer.
  2. Choose where to put it, or just click Next to accept the default. It installs for your account only, so it never asks for an administrator password.
  3. Click Finish. You will now have a SpaceWise shortcut on your desktop and in the Start Menu.
  4. The first time you open it, Windows may show a blue box saying "Windows protected your PC". Click More info, then Run anyway. SpaceWise is not signed with a paid certificate yet, and that box is simply what an unsigned program looks like.

That warning is also what an unwanted program looks like. Only run an installer you got from somewhere you trust.

There is no licence to accept, no account to create, and nothing to sign in to.

A version you can put on a USB stick

SpaceWise-0.1.0-x64.exe is a portable copy. It needs no installation and leaves nothing behind — useful for a memory stick or a computer you would rather not change. It creates no shortcut, and your settings and scan history still live in your own user folder.

Updating

Version checks are manual. SpaceWise never downloads or installs anything on its own: you ask it to check, and you approve the download and the install separately. (Help → Check for updates.)

3. Your first scan

Open SpaceWise. On the opening screen you have three ways to choose what to scan:

  1. Click Scan next to a drive, to scan the whole thing. This is the honest starting point if you do not know what is filling it up.
  2. Or click Scan a folder… and pick one folder. (You can also press Ctrl+O at any time.)
  3. Or drag a folder from File Explorer and drop it anywhere on the SpaceWise window.

The first scan of a large drive takes a while, because it is reading real information about every file rather than guessing.

You can stop at any point, with Esc or the Cancel button. Stopping keeps everything measured so far and marks the result as incomplete — it does not pretend you are looking at the whole picture. F5 scans again.

4. What you are looking at

Most of the window is the map. Each block is a file or a folder; bigger means more space. Click a block to zoom into it, and the path along the top tells you where you are. Folders too small to draw are gathered into a single block rather than dropped, so the map always accounts for everything.

Down the right-hand side are the tabs, which are the different ways of looking at the same scan:

TabWhat it is for
TreeThe folder-by-folder list, with sizes. Expand a folder with the arrow beside its name.
LargestThe biggest individual files in the scan, biggest first.
TypesHow much space each kind of file takes — videos, archives, images and so on. Click a type to see just those files.
Clean upWhere SpaceWise suggests things to remove, and where you approve them.
DuplicatesFiles with identical contents.
ChangesWhat changed since an earlier scan of the same folder. (Paid.)

Two sizes, and why the difference matters

Every file has two figures:

  • Logical size — what the file "is": the number you would see in the file's Properties.
  • On-disk size — what it actually occupies, rounded up to the disk's blocks.

A folder of ten thousand tiny files can take up far more than the sum of those files' sizes, because each one rounds up to its own block. Wherever SpaceWise is talking about space you could get back, it shows the on-disk figure, and says which figure it is showing. When the two differ a lot, that is usually the answer to "why is this folder eating my drive".

What it cannot see

A file locked by another program, or a folder your account is not allowed to open, is not silently skipped — it is counted as unreadable, the total is marked as having gaps, and you can click the Issues figure at the bottom to see each one with the code Windows returned. If you scan C:\Windows as an ordinary user, expect a number of these.

5. Finding the big files

Open the Largest tab. You get the biggest files in the scan, largest first, with the folder each one lives in.

To remove some:

  1. Tick the box on the left of each file you want gone. Tick as many as you like — the bar at the top keeps a running count and tells you how much they add up to.
  2. Or click Select all to tick everything in the list at once.
  3. Click Send to Recycle Bin.
  4. SpaceWise shows you the list and the total size. Click Send to Recycle Bin there to confirm, or Cancel to back out.

You can also remove one file on its own: click the small bin icon on its row, or select the row and press Delete.

Everything goes to the Recycle Bin, so a mistake is recoverable — restore it the same way you would any deleted file. SpaceWise only ever deletes for good if you tick the permanent box in the Clean up review, and that tick is never remembered.

The Found tab works exactly the same way, so you can search for something like .iso, tick the results and clear them in one go.

6. Cleaning up safely

Open the Clean up tab. It proposes candidates — temporary files, caches, build output and similar — and the order of what happens next is always the same:

  1. Pick. Nothing is ticked for you. Every item is your decision. Tick whole groups or single folders, then click Review and clean.
  2. Check. SpaceWise looks at each item again, right then: is it still there, is it still the same size, has it been modified? Anything that changed is refused and left exactly where it is. This is what stops a stale list from removing something you edited while the window was open.
  3. Review. You see what will happen, item by item, with the size of each, and a list of anything that was left out and why.
  4. Confirm. Click Remove… to go ahead.
  5. Watch. While it works you see how far it has got — "12 of 40 done" with a bar underneath — and which item it is on. If you want it to stop, click Cancel or press Esc. Anything already removed stays removed; everything it had not reached is listed as skipped, and the run is reported as incomplete rather than as a clean sweep.
  6. Report. Removed, failed and skipped are listed separately, each with the reason. Anything left behind means the run is marked incomplete.

Every run is recorded. Click History on the Clean up tab to see past runs — how much each freed, and, where something was refused, every path and the reason for it.

What is never offered

Windows itself, Program Files, ProgramData, the Recycle Bin, System Volume Information and the boot and recovery folders are refused outright, even if you scanned a drive that contains them. So are things like .git folders, SSH and GPG keys, and your browser's saved passwords and cookies. If a folder appears in the review and then in the "left out, and why" list, that is this working.

7. Finding duplicate files

Open the Duplicates tab. Choose the smallest file worth considering, then click Look for duplicates.

It works in two passes, to stay reasonably quick: it compares the beginning and the end of each file first, and only reads a whole file when those already match. Files are compared by their actual contents, never by their names.

The figure it shows is what you would actually get back: all the copies, minus the one it keeps, counted by the space they occupy.

To de-duplicate:

  1. Each set is a heading — the size and how many copies — with the copies listed under it.
  2. Click the small bin icon on the copies you want to remove, keeping one of each set.
  3. As soon as a set is down to one copy it disappears from the list, and the "wasted" figure at the top drops.

Twenty sets at a time without a licence. The duplicate list shows the twenty sets worth the most and tells you how many it left out and how much they hold. Clearing the ones you can see brings the rest up, so you can still work through everything — a licence simply shows them all at once. Finding duplicates is free either way.

Two things it will not offer you. A file whose contents are shared with another name on disk — a "hard link" — is left out, because deleting one of those names frees nothing. And a file it cannot read is left alone rather than assumed to be safe.

8. Searching, and narrowing by size or age

  1. Press Ctrl+F. The search box along the top takes the cursor.
  2. Type part of a name. Results appear in the Found tab as you type, biggest first.
  3. Narrow it further with the two lists beside the search box: Larger than (a minimum size) and Untouched for (an age).
  4. Click the small × to clear the search and go back.

Search works on a finished scan. If you press Ctrl+F while a scan is still running, SpaceWise tells you so rather than doing nothing — the results would be read from a list that is being rewritten every second.

9. Keeping a scan up to date

The Live button on the toolbar keeps the scan in step with the disk: Windows tells SpaceWise which folders changed, and only those are read again. Try it on a folder you have open in another window — save a file there and it appears in the map.

Two things to know:

  • It needs a finished scan. On a scan you stopped, the button is greyed out, and hovering it says why.
  • It gives up rather than falling behind: if changes come faster than Windows can report them, it stops and tells you, and Scan again catches up.

Switching it off saves the current state to your history, and says so — including if that save did not work.

11. Free and paid

Everything that touches your files is free, and never expires. Scanning, the map, the folder tree, the type breakdown, searching, reviewed cleanup with the full safety model, your history and duplicate detection are all free. Putting the safety model behind a paywall would make the free version dangerous, so it is not behind one.

A licence adds three things:

  • Comparing scans — what changed between two scans of the same folder, in the Changes tab.
  • Unlimited duplicate results — without it the duplicate list shows twenty sets at a time (see section 7).
  • The optional written summary, and the Ask button beside a finding.

Where to put your licence. Open Help → Licence, paste the key you were given and confirm. There is no account and nothing is sent anywhere — the check happens on your own computer, immediately.

If a licence expires, it keeps working for a fortnight's grace, so a laptop that has been offline does not lose features it paid for. After that the paid features stop, with a clear message — and your files, settings, history and all the free features are untouched. An expired licence never locks you out of your own data.

The optional written summary

This is the one feature that can talk to something outside your computer, and it is off until you set it up. You give it a web address, one or more model names, and an API key if your service wants one. Then it writes a short description of your scan in words.

Before anything is sent, SpaceWise shows you the exact text, word for word, above the button that sends it. It is short — how many files, how big, which folders hold the most, and a breakdown by type. It does not include your file contents, and it does not include file names unless you tick the box for names.

It cannot delete, select or recommend deleting anything. It writes prose, shown beside your findings, and there is no path from that text to an action.

12. Where your information lives

Everything SpaceWise keeps is in your own user folder, at %APPDATA%\SpaceWise:

ItemWhat it is
snapshots\One file per scan, so your history and the Changes tab work
cleanup-audit.jsonlA record of every cleanup, one line per action
license.keyYour licence, if you have one
ai-summary.jsonYour summary address and key, if you set one up

Delete that folder and SpaceWise forgets everything — it will not touch your actual files. Nothing here is uploaded anywhere. There is no account, no telemetry and no analytics.

13. If something goes wrong

What you seeWhat it means
A drive is missing from the opening screen, or listed as one that could not be read If it is listed with a reason, Windows would not let SpaceWise read it (a disconnected network drive, a card reader with no card in it). If it is not listed at all, there is nothing on that drive letter.
A folder shows as unreadable Your account is not allowed to open it. This is normal for parts of C:\Windows and for other people's folders. It does not stop the scan.
A cleanup item was refused Something about it changed while the window was open, or it is a protected location. It was left alone deliberately — scan again and try once more.
A duplicate was not offered Either it is not really identical, or its contents are shared with another name on disk, or it could not be read. All three are deliberate.
A button is greyed out Hover it: there is a sentence saying why.
Something you wanted has gone Open the Recycle Bin and restore it. SpaceWise only ever moves files there, never deletes them outright, until you empty the bin yourself.
The written summary did not work Check the address is https for anything on the internet, and that the model name is one your service recognises. The text shown above the Send button is exactly what was sent, so it is the thing to compare against.

Two more things you might be told, both normal:

  • "Live sync stopped" — the disk changed faster than Windows could report. Scan again to catch up.
  • "This scan was stopped" — you cancelled it. What you see is what was measured before you stopped.

14. Removing it

  1. Open Settings and go to Apps → Installed apps.
  2. Find SpaceWise, click the ⋯ beside it and choose Uninstall.
  3. Confirm. (You can also run the uninstaller in the folder you installed it to.)

Uninstalling removes the program. It leaves your scan history and your licence behind, so a reinstall picks up where you left off. To remove those too, delete %APPDATA%\SpaceWise yourself.

15. Things worth knowing

Stated plainly, because finding out later is worse.

  • The program is not signed. Windows shows the "Windows protected your PC" warning the first time. Nothing else is affected and no feature is blocked.
  • Updates are never automatic, and they are not signed yet, so only accept one you were expecting.
  • Files linked to the same contents are counted once per name in the scan totals. That is standard for storage tools, and it makes folders such as WinSxS look bigger than the space they uniquely take up. The duplicates figure is not affected.
  • Large drives take time. SpaceWise uses all your processor's cores, but a drive with a million files takes tens of seconds at the very least, and longer on a busy machine.
  • The window itself is the least-tested part. The logic underneath is heavily tested; that a particular panel draws correctly is checked by hand. If something on screen looks wrong, please say so.
